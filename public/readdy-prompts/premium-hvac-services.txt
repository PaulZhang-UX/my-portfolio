2. 整站重建 Prompt
============================================================

Project Definition

Build a single-page residential HVAC service website for “Summit Home Services,” a family-owned heating, cooling and indoor air quality company serving Austin, Texas and nearby communities. The website must prioritize same-day repair bookings, new-system estimates, seasonal maintenance and a clear local-service trust story. This is a front-end marketing and lead-capture experience, not a full booking database, CRM, payment system or customer portal.

Global Visual Direction

Create a premium, practical and trustworthy local-service brand. Use a predominantly white canvas with deep navy #0B1B3B, technical blue #2C55B8, dark blue #22459A and ember red #C8322A. Use blue for trust, technical clarity, links and interactive indicators. Use ember red for same-day urgency, emergency cues and primary booking actions. Use Sora for bold display headings and Inter for body copy, labels and forms. Use high-quality residential HVAC photography with clean technicians, service vans, thermostats, indoor units, outdoor equipment and bright Austin homes. Avoid generic abstract SaaS styling, excessive glassmorphism, dark-only layouts, neon colors and decorative elements that reduce service clarity.

Design System

Use a centered max-width of approximately 1400px with 24px mobile side padding and 40px desktop side padding. Use rounded-md buttons, rounded-xl or rounded-2xl cards, subtle 1px slate borders, restrained shadows and generous section spacing. Use Sora at weight 800 for large headings, usually with tight tracking and compact line-height. Use Inter at 14px to 17px for body copy. Use uppercase eyebrow labels with 0.18em to 0.22em letter spacing. Alternate white and slate-50 sections. Use deep navy for the dark navigation row, same-day band, booking CTA and footer. Keep all important actions visually explicit and keyboard accessible.

Website Information Architecture

Implement one real page at /. The wildcard route should render a simple not-found page. The home page is a long scroll narrative with these same-page anchors: #services, #systems, #how, #reviews, #financing, #area and #book. The main navigation links to those anchors. A 24/7 Emergency label may be shown in the navigation, but do not invent a separate emergency page unless one is provided.

Section-by-Section Layout

1. Navbar

Purpose: establish the brand, proof, phone contact and primary conversion action before the visitor scrolls.

Structure: Use a fixed header. The first white row is 56px high and contains a snowflake-style blue icon, the Summit Home Services wordmark, the subtitle “HEATING · COOLING · AIR QUALITY,” desktop trust claims, review score and a telephone link. The second desktop-only row is 52px high with a deep navy background. Place six links for Services, Systems, How it works, Reviews, Financing and Service Area. On the right, show “24/7 Emergency” and an ember “Book Service” button. At below-large breakpoints, show a menu button and a white vertical mobile menu with the same links and a blue booking button.

Behavior: Add a subtle shadow when scrollY is greater than 40px. Smooth-scroll to hash targets with an offset based on the actual header height. Compute the active section based on its position relative to the header and show a short white underline on active desktop navigation. Close the mobile menu after navigation.

2. Hero

Purpose: make the same-day, new-system and year-round-care offers immediately understandable while creating a memorable HVAC visual.

Desktop layout: Use a full-viewport, minimum 720px high stage with a residential background image, a local white readability glow, a left-aligned copy block and two foreground image layers on the right: a service van or HVAC equipment layer and a technician layer. Add an Austin, TX pill, a scene eyebrow, a large two-line Sora headline with a blue accent phrase, a short explanation, two CTAs and proof points for licensing, same-day service, upfront pricing and reviews. Show a vertical scene indicator at lower left and “SCROLL” with a bouncing arrow at lower right.

Scenes:

- Scene 01: “01 · SAME-DAY REPAIR”; headline “Comfort, delivered today.”; copy about fast, honest Austin repair within hours; primary CTA “Book Same-Day Repair”; secondary CTA is a phone action labeled “See How It Works” in the source.
- Scene 02: “02 · NEW SYSTEM”; headline “Better comfort for what’s next.”; copy about high-efficiency heat pumps and complete systems; CTA “Get Free Estimate”; secondary CTA “See Financing.”
- Scene 03: “03 · YEAR-ROUND CARE”; headline “One tune-up, a whole season easier.”; copy about seasonal maintenance, air-quality checks and smart thermostat tuning; CTA “Start Maintenance Plan”; secondary CTA “Learn More.”

Desktop interaction: On initial load, lock document scrolling at the top. Intercept wheel events and relevant paging keys. A downward wheel advances to the next scene; an upward wheel returns to the previous scene. Ignore small wheel deltas and prevent rapid re-entry during transitions. After the third scene, release normal page scrolling. If the user clicks a non-empty in-page hash link, release the Hero lock so the anchor can be reached. When the user scrolls back to the very top, allow the lock to be restored.

Scene transition sequence: fade/translate the technician out first, move the previous vehicle/equipment layer out, swap the background after the outgoing vehicle phase, fade the previous background over 500ms, then enter the new equipment/vehicle and technician. The source uses approximately 300ms technician-out, 850ms vehicle-out, 500ms background swap, 850ms vehicle-in, 350ms technician delay and 450ms technician entrance for the first scene. Scene 02 and 03 use tighter technician timing around 260ms to 320ms and specialized equipment entrance around 400ms. Use cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1) and ease-out where appropriate. Animate new scene eyebrow, headline, paragraph and CTA group with fade-up staggered by about 0ms, 120ms and 220ms.

Mobile behavior: below 1024px, remove the document scroll lock and render a normal min-height section. Show only the first repair scene with the background image, white gradient wash, copy above a lower vehicle/technician composition, stacked CTAs and three compact proof points. Keep the Austin pill and accessible phone link.

7. Comfort System / System Upgrade

Purpose: explain that comfort is a coordinated system, not a single machine, and guide visitors to a new-system estimate.

12. Booking CTA and form

Otherwise serialize the fields as URL-encoded data and POST to the supplied Readdy form endpoint. 13. Footer

Purpose: provide final brand context, secondary links and legal placeholders.

Layout: Use deep navy. Show the blue snowflake-style mark, Summit Home Services, the Austin since 2003 statement, social icon buttons, and three columns for Services, Company and Contact. End with a thin divider, copyright, TACLA #48120, licensed/insured statement and Privacy, Terms and Accessibility placeholders.

Content and Image Direction

Use the exact content hierarchy described above. Keep the tone direct, neighborly and operational: same-day, local dispatcher, stocked trucks, upfront quote, clean in-home service and predictable comfort. Use image subjects that map directly to the service story. Preserve the supplied Hero layer images as independent background/equipment/technician layers when available. If remote image APIs are unavailable, substitute equivalent editorial residential HVAC imagery but retain subject, crop, light and aspect ratio.

Interaction and Motion Behavior

Implement only the behaviors supported by the source audit. Prefer CSS transitions and small React state machines. Use IntersectionObserver for one-time reveal and number animations. Use fixed staged timers only for the Hero scene state machine. Provide reduced-motion behavior in a production implementation: disable scroll hijacking and shorten nonessential transitions when prefers-reduced-motion is enabled.

Responsive Design

At desktop width, use the two-row navigation and three-scene Hero. At widths below 1024px, use the mobile Hero and mobile menu. Collapse major grids to one column, turn the system card stack into a pager, stack forms, wrap trust metrics and keep all buttons at least comfortably tappable. Preserve image readability and do not allow technician or equipment layers to overlap the header.

Technical Requirements

Use React, TypeScript, Vite and Tailwind CSS or equivalent utility classes. Keep the home page and component boundaries clear. Use semantic sections and labels. Use real anchor targets. Load Inter and Sora or a close local fallback. Use Remix Icon or an equivalent icon set. Keep all external image, map and form dependencies explicit and replaceable. Do not add a CMS, database, payment flow, authenticated account area or invented pages.

Quality Assurance

Verify that the route / renders the complete section order. Verify that all navigation anchors except the documented #emergency target existing sections. Verify the Hero can move forward and backward, releases scroll after scene 03 and does not trap mobile scrolling. Verify all service, system, financing and booking CTAs reach #book or the intended anchor. Verify the system pager, Why accordion, review carousel, count-up and form states. Verify layout at desktop, tablet and mobile widths. Verify remote assets have alt text where meaningful, decorative Hero layers are empty-alt, and keyboard users can operate menu, accordion, pager, carousel and form controls.

Explicit Constraints / Avoidances

Do not redesign the site into a different visual direction. Do not replace the local-service narrative with generic SaaS or ecommerce patterns. Do not describe the Hero as merely “smooth animation”; implement its scene order, scroll lock, staged layers and timing. Do not present external Readdy form delivery as a local backend. Do not claim actual appointment inventory, CRM persistence, payment processing or authenticated user accounts. Do not create independent routes that are not in the source. Do not hard-code inaccessible interaction states. Do not omit the Austin/local proof, same-day promise, financing and Comfort Club content.
